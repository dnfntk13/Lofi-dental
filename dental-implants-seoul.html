How dental implants replace missing teeth

A dental implant is placed in the jaw to support a replacement tooth. A single implant can support a crown; several missing teeth may be restored with an implant-supported bridge or denture. The restoration and the implant are different parts of the overall plan.

Tell the dentist which gaps concern you, how they affect eating or appearance, and whether you already wear a bridge or denture. Ask how the proposed replacement would fit alongside your remaining teeth.

Assessment before implant treatment

Your dental implant consultation considers oral health and medical history. General health, tobacco use and conditions that affect healing matter when discussing surgery. An examination is needed before suitability can be determined.

Bring a list of your medicines and information about previous dental treatment. If you have existing dental records, ask the clinic how to share them securely. Questions about alternatives, the planned restoration and follow-up are part of the consultation, even if you have already decided that you want to replace a missing tooth.

Placement, healing and the final restoration

Implant treatment commonly involves placement, a healing period and fitting the replacement tooth or teeth. Bone healing around an implant takes time, and the timing of the final restoration varies by case. Do not assume a complete implant treatment can fit into one short trip.

Ask for a schedule that distinguishes the consultation, surgical stage, restoration appointments and review visits. Also ask what temporary arrangements may be appropriate while you wait, and whom to contact with concerns between appointments.

Planning dental implants in Gangnam as an overseas visitor

If you are traveling to Korea for dental care, share the dates of your stay and your ability to return. A useful plan addresses follow-up as clearly as the first appointment. Before booking travel around treatment, discuss what would happen if the schedule needs to change.

At lofi in Apgujeong, an English consultation gives you an opportunity to clarify each stage. If several areas need treatment, read our full mouth restoration guide to prepare questions about coordinating care.

Questions to bring to your appointment

Which replacement options should I compare? How many treatment stages are expected? What could change the schedule? How should I maintain the restoration? What follow-up is needed after I leave Seoul? Write down your priorities so you can discuss them before choosing a treatment plan.
