What is full mouth restoration?

Full mouth restoration is also described as full mouth reconstruction or full mouth rehabilitation. It considers extensive dental problems together, such as multiple missing teeth, damaged restorations or worn teeth. The goal is a coordinated plan for function and appearance.

The term does not by itself say which procedures you need. Ask the dentist to explain the findings, which areas require attention and how each proposed step contributes to the overall plan.

A plan for worn, damaged or missing teeth

Depending on the examination, comprehensive restorative dentistry may involve crowns, bridges, veneers, implants or dentures. These are different options, not a checklist of procedures every patient receives.

Start by describing everyday problems: difficulty chewing, teeth that feel worn, concerns about old dental work or changes in your smile. Bring your treatment history and explain what has and has not worked well for you. This gives the consultation a practical focus.

Full mouth rehabilitation and smile makeover: the difference

A smile makeover usually begins with appearance goals. A full mouth restoration discussion considers broader restorative needs and how the teeth function together. There can be overlap, but the names do not replace an examination.

For missing teeth, our dental implants guide explains the questions to ask about replacement teeth and treatment stages. For concerns mainly about front-tooth appearance, explore veneer options. Your consultation can help separate these concerns and set priorities.

Planning treatment in stages

Before starting extensive dental treatment, ask for an explanation of the proposed sequence. Which concerns come first? Which existing teeth can be maintained? What decisions are still dependent on the examination? What maintenance and review appointments should you plan for?

A written list of questions can help you compare options without losing sight of your original priorities. Discuss comfort, function and appearance separately if it makes the choices easier to understand.
